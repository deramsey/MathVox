Quick-start guide

MathVox turns an equation into versions that screen readers, braille readers and listeners can use. Your equation is kept while you read this page.

In three steps

  1. Enter your equation in the equation box. Below it, the Type, LaTeX and Draw tabs offer other ways in: keyboard tips, a box for pasting LaTeX (then select Convert), or drawing by hand.
  2. Choose where it’s going, such as a Canvas page or a Word document (see the table below).
  3. Copy it with the main button (for an image, Download), then follow the short steps shown under it.

Reading styles

Screen readers will hear, under the equation, shows how it will be read. Switch between two styles there; the choice is also used for the description, Listen and the suggested alt text:

  • MathSpeak marks exactly where each part starts and ends: "StartFraction a plus 1 Over 2 EndFraction". Precise for complicated equations and familiar to many screen reader users.
  • ClearSpeak reads more like a teacher would: "the fraction with numerator a plus 1 and denominator 2". Easier to follow for most listeners.

Choosing Read or listen also shows your equation in both styles side by side, with a Listen button for each. NVDA, JAWS and Windows Narrator read math in ClearSpeak by default, so that column is close to what most screen reader users will hear.

"Say what it means"

Some notation means different things in different places. (0, 5) can be a point or an interval; |x| can be absolute value or the size of a set; y(t) can be a function or y times t. When you choose Canvas page, MathVox asks about each one it finds. Your answer is written into the MathML, so screen readers that support it (NVDA and JAWS with MathCAT) say "the open interval from 0 to 5" instead of guessing. Function choices also carry over to Word document.

Drawing by hand

Select the Draw tab under the equation box and write with a mouse, pen or finger. When you pause, MathVox shows what it read. Check it, then select Insert into Equation; it goes in where your cursor is in the equation field, so you can draw a long equation in parts. Recognition is good but not perfect, especially with look-alike characters (1, l and I; x and X), so fix anything it got wrong in the equation field. The first time, the recognition model takes a moment to download (about 17 MB).

Handy to know

  • Keyboard: everything works from the keyboard. Open All keyboard shortcuts on the Type tab for the full list, such as showing the on-screen math keyboard.
  • Clear (next to the equation box) empties it. If you clear by mistake, select Undo Clear before typing anything new.
  • Share a link: Copy share link copies a link that opens MathVox with your equation and format already filled in.
  • Settings stay: your last equation, format and display settings are remembered in your browser. See Privacy.
  • Display: Dark Mode and Dyslexia-Friendly Font are at the top of every page. MathVox also follows your system's high contrast settings.
